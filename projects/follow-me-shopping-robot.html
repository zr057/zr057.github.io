Mobile robotics · Planning and control

FollowMe Autonomous Shopping Cart

A two-stage indoor robotics project: an initial green folding-cart conversion exposed hardware-integration limits, so navigation, obstacle avoidance, and motion-control validation moved to a purpose-built four-wheel mobile platform.

View GitHub repository

Project outcome

The project began by adding drive hardware and LiDAR to a green folding shopping cart. Its consumer-grade folding structure, limited mounting rigidity, and inconsistent drive response made repeatable closed-loop experiments impractical. The conversion therefore remained an early integration attempt, and the team adopted a purpose-built four-wheel mobile platform for path-planning, trajectory-tracking, and corridor obstacle-avoidance tests.

  • Used the green cart to identify mechanical, sensor-mounting, and drive-control integration constraints.
  • Integrated LiDAR hardware and prepared occupancy-grid inputs for planning.
  • Implemented grid-based global planning and local obstacle avoidance.
  • Connected planning output with closed-loop chassis control.

Platform iteration

Why the project moved beyond the green cart

The green cart was useful for testing LiDAR placement, wiring, and drive installation, but it did not provide the rigidity or repeatable motion needed for controlled navigation experiments. Rather than overstate this attempt as a successful prototype, the project transferred the sensing, planning, and control workflow to the four-wheel mechanical platform.

System workflow

From environment representation to chassis motion

The verified planning workflow connects sensor inputs, occupancy-grid representation, global-local planning, and closed-loop control.

01

Sense

LiDAR and odometry capture the surrounding geometry and robot state.

02

Represent

A binary occupancy grid marks free space and obstacles for route generation.

03

Plan

A* generates the global route while local planning reacts to nearby obstacles.

04

Control

Closed-loop control converts path error into differential wheel commands.

Planning contribution

  • Eight-direction grid search with map-boundary and collision checks
  • Heuristic-guided node expansion and parent-based path reconstruction
  • Redundant-turn removal and collision-checked path shortcutting
  • Local trajectory scoring using goal direction, obstacle clearance, speed, and global-route consistency
