Research problem

Why cross-rate transfer matters

Fast-charging experiments are expensive and time-consuming, while models trained only on public low-rate or small-cell datasets may not generalize to large-format pouch cells. The study asks whether multi-source degradation knowledge can support accurate full-life SOH prediction with limited labeled high-rate data.

Experimental foundation

High-rate pouch-cell aging dataset

Three commercial LG NMC32 pouch cells were cycled in a temperature-controlled chamber. Voltage, current, capacity, and temperature were recorded across the aging process to form the target domain.

Research workflow

From raw cycling data to target-cell prediction

The workflow separates broad degradation-pattern learning from target-domain adaptation so that limited high-rate labels can be used efficiently.

01

Prepare data

Align cycling records and derive health indicators from voltage, current, capacity, and degradation behavior.

02

Screen features

Use Pearson correlation analysis to retain indicators that track capacity decline.

03

Pre-train

Learn shared temporal degradation patterns from eight cells across three public datasets.

04

Transfer and test

Freeze TCN parameters, tune the target model with GJO, and predict the full life of another pouch cell.

Model architecture

Complementary sequence modeling

Temporal Convolutional Network

Captures local degradation patterns with causal temporal convolutions.

Transformer Encoder

Models long-range dependencies across the battery aging sequence.

Golden Jackal Optimization

Searches key target-domain hyperparameters used during adaptation.

Transfer protocol

Cross-cell validation

One target cell supplies limited fine-tuning data, with a 90%/10% training-validation split described in the project materials. The adapted model then predicts another cell's full SOH trajectory.

Four directions were evaluated: Battery 1→2, Battery 1→3, Battery 2→1, and Battery 3→1.

Research contribution

What the study demonstrates

  • Multi-source pre-training can support cross-rate, cross-cell transfer.
  • Local and long-range temporal modeling are combined in one architecture.
  • Target-domain optimization supports adaptation with limited labeled data.
  • Performance is checked across four train-to-test cell directions.
Reproducibility

Public scope and boundaries

Selected MATLAB scripts cover the proposed architecture, baseline models, GJO functions, evaluation utilities, and plotting workflow.

Raw laboratory data, third-party datasets, trained weights, and the research code are not distributed through this public page. The page therefore documents the methodology and reported evidence rather than offering full end-to-end reproduction.
