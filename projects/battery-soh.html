Battery health · Transfer learning

Cross-Rate Lithium-Ion Battery SOH Prediction

A GJO-TCN-Transformer framework for transferring degradation knowledge from multi-source public datasets to high-rate pouch-cell state-of-health prediction.

Research question

Can knowledge learned from public battery datasets support accurate full-life SOH prediction when labeled high-rate automotive pouch-cell data are limited?

Approach

  • Pre-trained a source-domain model using eight public cells.
  • Used a TCN to capture local degradation patterns and a Transformer to model long-term dependencies.
  • Applied Golden Jackal Optimization to tune target-domain adaptation.
  • Evaluated four cross-battery transfer settings using three laboratory pouch cells.

Reported results

The proposed model achieved the lowest reported MAE, RMSE, and MAPE in all four transfer settings in the manuscript.

Research design

From public-cell knowledge to high-rate prediction

The study separates broad degradation-pattern learning from target-cell adaptation, allowing the model to use limited high-rate aging data more efficiently.

01

Source data

Health features were extracted from eight cells across NASA, CALCE, and XJTU datasets.

02

Feature screening

Pearson correlation analysis was used to retain degradation-sensitive health indicators.

03

Sequence learning

A TCN captured local temporal patterns while a Transformer modeled long-term dependencies.

04

Target adaptation

TCN parameters were frozen and GJO-guided fine-tuning adapted the model to a high-rate pouch cell.

Technical contribution

  • Connected multi-source pre-training with cross-rate, cross-cell transfer.
  • Combined local and long-range temporal feature modeling.
  • Used population-based optimization to support target-domain adaptation.
  • Validated the method under four train-to-test cell combinations.

Reproducibility scope

Selected MATLAB scripts cover the model architecture, baselines, optimization functions, evaluation utilities, and plotting workflow.

Raw laboratory data, third-party datasets, and trained weights are not distributed. Reported values are taken from the manuscript.
