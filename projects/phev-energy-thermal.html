Coordinated Energy and Battery Thermal Management for a PHEV

An end-to-end Python simulation that connects vehicle dynamics, component efficiency, battery state, and thermal constraints to compare rule-based, real-time, and globally optimized control strategies under CLTC-P.

Engineering question

How should engine and battery power be allocated when SOC and temperature both matter?

A strategy that minimizes instantaneous fuel use may draw heavily on the battery, while a conservative strategy can protect SOC at the cost of fuel. At high ambient temperature, the controller must also limit thermal stress. This project treats energy consumption and battery temperature as a coupled control problem.

System model

One simulation chain from the road load to battery temperature

The model converts a prescribed speed trace into wheel power demand, distributes power across the series-hybrid powertrain, and updates battery SOC and temperature at each time step.

Co-simulation architecture

Energy flow and heat generation are evaluated together

Engine power, battery power, SOC, heat generation, cooling power, and ambient heat transfer form a closed evaluation loop. This makes temperature a control variable rather than a result checked only after simulation.

01

Vehicle demand

Rolling resistance, aerodynamic drag, acceleration, and road grade determine traction power.

02

Powertrain

Fuel-rate and motor-efficiency maps convert commanded power into fuel and electrical energy.

03

Battery state

A simplified electrical model updates current and SOC while enforcing the permitted SOC range.

04

Thermal state

A first-order heat balance captures internal losses, ambient exchange, and active cooling.

Control design

Progressing from rules to optimization

Each controller answers the same power-allocation problem with a different level of system knowledge and computational demand.

Baseline

CD-CS

Uses SOC thresholds to switch from charge-depleting electric operation to charge-sustaining hybrid operation. It is transparent and easy to calibrate.

Online optimization

ECMS

Expresses electrical energy as equivalent fuel and minimizes instantaneous equivalent consumption. SOC feedback corrects the equivalence factor.

Thermal-aware online

Thermal ECMS

Adds a temperature-dependent battery-power penalty and cooling demand so the optimizer can trade energy efficiency against thermal protection.

Offline reference

Dynamic programming

Uses SOC and battery temperature as states and engine power as the decision variable. Full-cycle knowledge provides a global reference, not a directly deployable online controller.

Thermal-aware control changes both the battery trajectory and engine operation

Global benchmark

Dynamic programming reveals the value of future-cycle information

The offline solution anticipates upcoming load using the full speed cycle. Thermal ECMS is causal and more suitable for online implementation, but cannot plan with the same horizon.

Engineering reflection

What this project taught me

The most important lesson was that energy management cannot be judged by fuel consumption alone. Terminal SOC, battery temperature, constraint violations, and controller causality must be considered together. I also gained practical experience translating physical equations into a reproducible simulation, tuning interacting parameters, and interpreting results without overstating them.

Limitations & next steps

How I would extend the work

  • Calibrate component maps with measured vehicle data.
  • Compare strategies at matched terminal SOC.
  • Validate across additional cycles and temperatures.
  • Move from simplified thermal dynamics to a higher-fidelity battery model.
  • Evaluate real-time feasibility through HIL testing.
